We wish to submit our manuscript, **“A coherence diagnostic for hidden population structure in observational studies: a simulation study of stratification-based extraction”** (revised from *IONE: Incoherence-Oriented Neutralisation and Extraction for Detecting Hidden Population Structure in Observational Studies*), for consideration by *Journal of Causal Inference*.

**Fit with the journal.**
The manuscript studies a foundational causal-inference problem: hidden effect modification and confounding in observational studies can make a marginal treatment-effect estimate non-robust. We propose exploratory diagnostics (C1 and W) that quantify between- and within-stratum treatment-effect heterogeneity, and we benchmark stratification-based extraction against propensity-score, Gaussian-mixture and prognostic-score stratification in a large simulation. Although the work is methodological, it is framed explicitly in terms of causal estimands (the population risk-difference ATE) and the limits of recovery from observed proxies, which we believe aligns with the scope of *Journal of Causal Inference*.

**Revisions since the previous peer review.**
A previous version was reviewed by *BMC Medical Research Methodology* and rejected as not suitable for further revision. We have substantially revised the manuscript in response to all reviewer comments:
- Reframed the paper as an **exploratory diagnostic-sensitivity study** rather than a fully developed workflow.
- Introduced a binary treatment variable into the simulation and made confounding and effect modification explicit.
- Added three active comparators and unified the method taxonomy.
- Reconciled the bias-reduction formula and values; the ATE estimand is now the collapsible risk difference.
- Downgraded C1 claims, added calibration-curve analyses and removed the provisional 0.05 threshold.
- Split W into a simulation-only true-CATE metric and an estimated operational diagnostic.
- Implemented a discovery/evaluation split for outcome-informed stratification and quantified induced-bias sensitivity.
- Corrected the semi-synthetic-illustration claims and added a comparability discussion.
