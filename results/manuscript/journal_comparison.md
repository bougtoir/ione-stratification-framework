# Target-journal comparison for IONE

This note supports the final target-journal choice. The current primary target is *Statistical Methods in Medical Research* (SMMR), with *Journal of Causal Inference* (JoCI) and *Statistics in Medicine* (SiM) as alternatives.

## 1. Statistical Methods in Medical Research (SAGE) — PRIMARY

**Why it is the best fit.**
- Explicitly publishes methodological developments for medical and epidemiological observational studies.
- Strong emphasis on propensity-score methods, stratification, and risk adjustment in health research.
- The active comparators (propensity-score quintiles, prognostic-score stratification, GMM) are core methods in medical statistics, which SMMR readers know well.
- A simulation study benchmarking diagnostics for observational studies is squarely within the journal’s stated scope.

**Author guidelines (key points).**
- Article types: Original Articles, Review Articles, Tutorial articles; the manuscript is an Original Article.
- Abstract: unstructured or structured, typically 250 words; 250-word limit is safe.
- Word limit for Original Articles is not hard-capped, but concise manuscripts are preferred.
- Reference style: Vancouver (numbered in order of appearance).
- Figures/tables: should be cited in text and supplied as separate high-resolution files; captions at the end of the manuscript.
- Data and code availability statement required.
- Ethical approval / informed consent not applicable for simulation study.

## 2. Journal of Causal Inference (de Gruyter) — ALTERNATIVE 1

**Why it is a plausible alternative.**
- Focuses on causal inference methodology, including identification, estimation and diagnostics for hidden heterogeneity.
- The paper’s causal framing (binary treatment, confounding, effect modification, ATE bias) maps directly to JoCI’s scope.
- Less medical-application emphasis; more formal/philosophical causal-inference readership.

**Practical considerations.**
- Smaller, more specialised readership; may be better if the editors view the work as a methodological contribution to causal inference rather than medical statistics.
- Page/word limits are generally flexible but may require shorter articles.
- Open access options; reasonable APC if open access is chosen.

## 3. Statistics in Medicine (Wiley) — ALTERNATIVE 2

**Why it was considered.**
- Premier venue for statistical methods in medical research, including observational-study methods.
- High visibility; strong tradition of propensity-score and causal inference papers.
- The simulation benchmark and diagnostic framing fit the journal well.

**Practical considerations.**
- Highly competitive; the paper’s modest effect-extraction claims may be seen as too incremental.
- Longer review timelines on average.
- Still a strong fallback if SMMR declines.

## Recommendation

Submit to **Statistical Methods in Medical Research** first. The manuscript’s emphasis on benchmarking medical-statistics tools (propensity-score stratification, prognostic scores, GMM) in a diagnostic-sensitivity simulation is most naturally read by the SMMR audience. If rejected, *Journal of Causal Inference* is the best second choice because of the causal-estimand framing, and *Statistics in Medicine* remains a third high-visibility option.
